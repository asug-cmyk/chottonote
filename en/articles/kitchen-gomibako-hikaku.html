If you're shopping for a new kitchen trash can — because the one you have can't be opened without touching it, or because it doesn't match your local sorting rules — you'll run into a wide range of shapes: pedal-operated bins, simple ones you just push open, and units with a row of sorting drawers, all of which make comparison tricky. Kitchen trash cans broadly fall into three types: pedal-type (open and close without using your hands), flap-type (a simple lid that opens with a push), and sorting-drawer type (split into multiple compartments to match your local sorting categories) — and which one you choose changes your day-to-day convenience. Here we compare four products on five spec items: sorting, footprint, capacity, opening and care.

Pros and Cons of Kitchen Trash Cans

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • With a pedal-type can, you can open and close it without getting your hands dirty
  • Sorting-drawer types often match your local sorting categories right out of the box
  • You can choose a capacity that matches how much trash your household produces

Cons

  • Horizontal sorting types need more width
  • If you're not unhappy with your current capacity or sorting setup, replacing it isn't a high priority
  • Moving parts such as pedal or flap hinges can wear faster if handled roughly

How to Choose a Kitchen Trash Can: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Know the Lid and Opening Types

Pedal, flap (push to open) and drawer types differ in how they are used and how much space they need. The products in this article cover all three.

2

Ease of Sorting

To match your local sorting rules, look at how many compartments you can sort into. The method differs by product: drawers, buckets, or a slit in the body.

3

Match Capacity to Your Household's Waste

A small can needs emptying more often; a large one takes up room. For sorting types, look at both the total and the size of each compartment.

4

Space You Need

If it goes into a gap in the kitchen, compare the outer width × depth (footprint). Also leave room for the pedal or drawers to move, and check the height.

5

Ease of Cleaning

A trash can is used every day, so how easy it is to wash and to handle the bag decides whether you stay happy with it. We look at four things.

Who Kitchen Trash Cans Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, kitchen trash cans tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to open and close the bin without touching it
  • Want it to match your local council's sorting rules directly
  • Want a capacity matched to how much waste your household produces

Consider other options if you...

  • Are happy with your current bin's capacity and how easy it is to sort into
  • Put durability of the moving parts first (pedals and hinges can wear with long use)
  • Have limited space and cannot give up the width a sorting drawer unit needs

Handling Tips for Moving Parts, Drawers and Stainless Steel

In general, the moving parts that connect the pedal or lid to the body (a metal rod or plastic hinge) can loosen or snap if forced, so operating them gently helps them last. For sorting-drawer types, avoid overloading them with heavy trash and empty them regularly to spare the tabs and hooks on the drawers. For stainless steel models, wiping off moisture after use is a good precaution.

First Steps After You Buy

After setting it up, testing the pedal or lid's movement a few times and checking for sticking or unusual noise helps you catch any early defects. For sorting types, tracking the actual amount of trash you produce for about a week makes it easier to judge whether each compartment's capacity fits your household. For stainless steel models, quickly wiping off moisture after use is a good precaution.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
