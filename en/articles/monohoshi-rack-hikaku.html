About the scoresThe scores are not from hands-on testing. They are calculated mechanically by applying the criteria table at the end of the page to figures published on the manufacturers' official pages and manuals (and on retail or comparison pages where an official figure was not available). We call it the "Spec Score". It does not reflect how a product feels to use.

If you can't dry laundry outside during pollen season or the rainy season, or your balcony is too small to fit a full load, searching for an indoor drying rack quickly turns up a mix of shapes and setups — floor-standing folding towers, tension-rod pole-holders that wedge between ceiling and floor, panel types that fold down thin for storage, and large-capacity models that can dry a whole family's laundry — which makes it hard to know where to start. Indoor drying racks generally fall into four types: folding tower (the standard floor-standing style), tension-rod pole-holder (wedges between ceiling and floor and uses a standard pole), folding panel (a compact type that flattens for storage), and large-capacity multi-function (caster-equipped, able to dry a family's worth of laundry or even futons). Which type you pick changes how much you can dry at once and how easy it is to find a place to put it. Here we compare four products on five points: weight capacity, storability, ease of setup, light weight and extra features.

Pros and Cons of Indoor Drying Racks

Here's a rundown of the benefits of adding an indoor drying rack, along with a few points worth keeping in mind before you buy.

Pros

  • Lets you keep doing laundry indoors even during pollen season or the rainy season
  • You can choose a setup style: tower, tension-rod, panel, or large-capacity
  • With a type like the panel style that flattens for storage, finding a place to keep it when not in use is less of a problem
  • A large-capacity model can dry a family's laundry, even futons, all at once

Cons

  • If you're unsure about the material or strength of your ceiling or walls, it's safer to skip a tension-rod pole-holder
  • If you're already happy with your current rack's capacity and location, replacing it isn't a high priority
  • If you regularly hang large amounts of thick blankets or futons, you may exceed the weight capacity (7.5–15 kg across these four products)

Points to Know Before You Use It

Weight capacity is stated differently from product to product. Yamazaki Tower 6619 is 7.5 kg for the hanger pipe, Tenma Panel S is about 12 kg overall (about 4 kg per panel), and Iris Ohyama KTM-2018R is about 15 kg overall (about 4 kg per pole). Look at the per-panel or per-pole figure as well as the total, so you can spread laundry across several panels or poles.

For the tension pole-holder, Heian Shindo TMH-61, the maker says to install it where the base is solid and to check the pressure fit 2–3 days after installation. The caster-equipped KTM-2018R is described on its retail page as having locking casters that make it easy to move and to hold in place.

First Steps After You Buy

After setup, use it with a lighter-than-usual load for the first few days and check that no extra strain is placed on the wall or ceiling. For the tension pole-holder, follow the maker's guidance: check the pressure fit 2–3 days after installation and increase the pressure if it feels weak. For the tower type, which is assembled with the two included hex wrenches, check that the screws are firmly tightened after assembly to cut down on wobbling when you first start using it.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
