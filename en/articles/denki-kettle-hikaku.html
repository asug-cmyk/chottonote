Whether it takes forever to boil water, your current kettle has gotten old and started dripping from the spout, or it just can't hit the right temperature for coffee, searching for a replacement electric kettle usually turns up a wall of similar-looking products that are hard to tell apart. Electric kettles mainly differ on five things: capacity (around 0.8 L for one or two people versus around 1.2 L for three or more people or cooking that needs a lot of hot water), boiling power (rated power), temperature control and keep-warm (so you can set the right temperature for coffee or tea), safety features (spill resistance if tipped, a low-steam design and so on), and cord length. Here we score four products on these five items and compare them.

Pros and Cons of Electric Kettles

Before deciding to switch, here's a rundown of the benefits of an electric kettle and a few things to keep in mind.

Pros

  • You can pick a capacity that matches your household size, so you're not wasting water or energy
  • A model with temperature control and keep-warm function lets you enjoy coffee or tea at your preferred temperature
  • Models with strong safety features, such as a spill-resistant structure if it tips over or a low-steam design, let you use it with more peace of mind

Cons

  • If you already have a higher-end model with temperature control and you're happy with it, there's not much reason to switch
  • If you live alone and only boil small amounts, even an 0.8L model can feel too big
  • If electric kettles simply don't fit your lifestyle, this isn't a priority

How to Choose an Electric Kettle: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Match Capacity to Your Household and Water Use

For one or two people, around 0.8 L is a workable guide; for three or more, or if you also boil lots of water for cooking such as pasta, around 1.2 L is easier to live with. A larger kettle boils more at once, but it is heavier and needs more room.

Related pickTiger PCT-N120 / T-fal KO5903JPA（Capacity 5/5）

2

Look at Boiling Power (Rated Power)

The higher the rated power, the faster a kettle tends to boil the same amount. It matters if you want hot water right away on a busy morning. Actual boiling time varies with water amount, water temperature and room temperature.

Related pickTiger PCT-N120（Boiling power 5/5）

3

Decide Whether You Need Temperature Control or Keep-Warm

Coffee, black tea and green tea taste different depending on water temperature. If you want to brew at your preferred temperature, temperature control is handy, but it adds controls and usually raises the price. If boiling is enough, a basic model will do.

4

Check the Safety Features

Especially in homes with small children or pets, spill resistance if tipped and a low-steam design add peace of mind. We look at these five.

Related pickTiger PCT-N120 / T-fal KO5903JPA（Safety features 5/5）

5

Cord Length and Where It Can Sit

A kettle is used near an outlet, so cord length decides where you can put it. With a short cord you may need an extension lead.

Related pickT-fal KO5903JPA / Iris Ohyama IKEB800W（Cord length 5/5）

6

Pouring and Ease of Cleaning

You use a kettle every day, so the spout and how easy it is to wash also decide whether you keep using it. These are hard to compare in numbers, so they are not scored; here is what to look at.

Who Electric Kettles Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, electric kettles tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to match the capacity to your household size and not boil more than you use
  • Want to make coffee or tea at the temperature you prefer
  • Care about safety features such as a spill-resistant structure or a low-steam design

Consider other options if you...

  • Already own and are happy with a higher-end model with temperature control
  • Live alone and boil so little that even a 0.8L model feels large
  • Barely use an electric kettle as part of your routine

What to Look at When Choosing

For this comparison, we used the following five items of the Spec Score (weights: capacity 20, boiling power 20, temperature control / keep-warm 20, safety features 30, cord length 10).

1. Capacity (around 0.8 L for one or two people; around 1.2 L for three or more people, or cooking that uses a lot of hot water)

2. Boiling power (rated power; higher tends to boil faster)

3. Temperature control and keep-warm (can you set a temperature suited to coffee or tea, and can it keep water warm afterward?)

4. Safety features (auto shut-off, boil-dry protection, spill resistance if tipped, low-steam design, thermal fuse)

5. Cord length (how freely you can place it)

Spout shape and ease of cleaning are also worth looking at, but they are hard to compare in numbers, so they are not part of the score.

Commonly Cited Complaints, and How to Handle Them

Across electric kettles in general, commonly cited issues include steam leaking from around the lid and water dripping from the spout (we did not collect reviews for this article). When pouring, avoid tilting the kettle too steeply and keep the spout close to the water's surface — that helps cut down on dripping. Resin bodies can have a distinct smell when first used; boiling and discarding plain water once or twice after purchase, and airing the kettle out with the lid open, usually helps reduce it. Gaskets and filters can cause leaks or transfer odors as they wear, so on removable-type kettles it's worth taking them out regularly to wash and letting them dry completely before putting them back.

First Steps After You Buy

For the first use, don't dry-boil it — fill it with plain water, boil it once, and discard the water. This helps remove any resin smell or fine dust left over from manufacturing. If your model has safety features like a spill-resistant structure or a low-steam design, it's reassuring to check how the lock feels and how steam is released before you rely on them. For models with temperature control and keep-warm function, it's worth checking with a thermometer once that the displayed temperature roughly matches the actual water temperature, so you can start using it with confidence.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
