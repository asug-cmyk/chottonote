About the scoresThe scores are not from hands-on testing. They are calculated mechanically by applying the criteria table at the end of the page to figures published on the manufacturers' official pages and manuals (and on retail or comparison pages where an official figure was not available). We call it the "Spec Score". It does not reflect how a product feels to use.

If you're searching for storage cases because your clothes don't fit neatly in the closet, or because there's unused space at the top or bottom of your oshiire that you're not making the most of, you'll run into everything from hard plastic drawer units to soft non-woven cases, in a wide range of types and prices that make comparison difficult. Storage cases broadly fall into three types: hard plastic drawer units (designed to be stacked), foldable containers (collapsible when not in use), and non-woven soft cases (generally light, and easier to fit into gaps like under a bed). Which type you pick changes how stackable it is and how much effort it takes to get things in and out. Here we score four products on five items: stacking, capacity, access, fit in gaps and extra features.

Pros and Cons of Closet Storage Cases

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Lets you consolidate clothing and make better use of closet or oshiire space
  • Stackable types use vertical space too, boosting storage capacity
  • Non-woven types are generally light and fit into gaps like under a bed more easily

Cons

  • If a shelf's weight capacity is low or its depth is shallow, you'll need to check what size actually fits beforehand
  • Hard plastic drawer types are generally heavier, which can make getting things in and out feel like a bit of a chore
  • If you're already satisfied with the capacity of what you're using, replacing it may not be a high priority

Who Closet Storage Cases Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, closet storage cases tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to use the space in a closet or built-in cupboard efficiently
  • Want to stack and make use of the vertical space too
  • Also want gap storage, such as under a bed

Consider other options if you...

  • Are happy with the capacity of the storage cases you already use
  • Have a constrained space — a low shelf weight limit or shallow depth, for instance (check the dimensions before buying)
  • Want to store a lot of heavy clothing (load ratings differ by product: the L-53 is about 5.4 kg per drawer, the Yamazen SFOB-S 5 kg per tier)

What to Check Before You Buy

Measure the height, depth and width of the spot first and compare them with each product's outer size. If you plan to stack, also check the load rating of the shelf or floor and each product's own rating (the L-53 is about 5.4 kg per drawer and about 16 kg for a 2-high stack; the Yamazen SFOB-S is 5 kg per tier). The Yamazen SFOB-S casters are fixed and cannot swivel (per the retail page), so keep that in mind if you will move it often after setting it up.

First Steps After You Buy

For drawer-type products, open and close the empty drawer once before loading it with clothes, so you notice anything odd early. For products with casters, it is a good idea to check for any wobble once it is in place.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
