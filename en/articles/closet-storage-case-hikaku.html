If you're searching for storage cases because your clothes don't fit neatly in the closet, or because there's unused space at the top or bottom of your oshiire that you're not making the most of, you'll run into everything from hard plastic drawer units to soft non-woven cases, in a wide range of types and prices that make comparison difficult. Storage cases broadly fall into three types: hard plastic drawer units (designed to be stacked), foldable containers (collapsible when not in use), and non-woven soft cases (lightweight, fitting into gaps like under a bed). Which type you pick changes how stackable it is and how much effort it takes to get things in and out. Here we compare four products by stacking stability, ease of access, and how well they fit their intended spot.

Pros and Cons of Closet Storage Cases

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Lets you consolidate clothing and make better use of closet or oshiire space
  • Stackable types use vertical space too, boosting storage capacity
  • Non-woven types are lightweight and fit into gaps like under a bed

Cons

  • If a shelf's weight capacity is low or its depth is shallow, you'll need to check what size actually fits beforehand
  • Hard plastic drawer types have some heft, which can make getting things in and out feel like a bit of a chore
  • If you're already satisfied with the capacity of what you're using, replacing it may not be a high priority

Who Closet Storage Cases Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, closet storage cases tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to use the space in a closet or built-in cupboard efficiently
  • Want to stack and make use of the vertical space too
  • Also want gap storage, such as under a bed

Consider other options if you...

  • Are happy with the capacity of the storage cases you already use
  • Have a constrained space — a low shelf weight limit or shallow depth, for instance (check the dimensions before buying)
  • Want to store a lot of heavy clothing (non-woven types have no base panel and lose their shape, so a hard case suits better)

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Stacking stability (the maximum number of tiers you can stack, and whether it sags under a stack)

2. Ease of access (a drawer-style pull, or a lid you open and lift items out from above)

3. Material and weight suited to the spot you'll place it (a hard case for durability, or non-woven fabric for lightness)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

With hard plastic drawer types, molding variation between units can leave the drawer fitting the body tightly, requiring some real force to open and close. After buying, it helps to test opening and closing the empty drawer once — catching a snag early is easier to deal with than discovering it later. Non-woven types have no bottom panel, so packing them full of heavy clothing tends to leave them out of shape; they're better suited to holding mostly lighter items. Foldable container types often come with casters that can't swivel, so it's best not to rely on them too heavily if you plan on moving the case around frequently once it's in place.

First Steps After You Buy

For hard plastic types, it helps to test the drawer's action once before actually loading it with clothes — if it catches, working it gently a few times can loosen it up. For non-woven types, spreading heavier clothing across the bottom or lower layer, rather than piling it up, helps keep the bottom from sagging too much and losing its shape. For products with casters, it's a good idea to check for any wobble once it's in place.
