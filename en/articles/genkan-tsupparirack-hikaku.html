If your entryway is running out of storage for shoes, coats, umbrellas and the like, but you rent and do not want to drill into the wall, a tension rack is a natural thing to look for. These racks wedge in place between two walls or between floor and ceiling, so the basic appeal is that you can install one without drilling. The supported size range and weight capacity vary by product, though, so check beforehand that the measurement of your spot (the width between walls, or the floor-to-ceiling height) falls within the range. Pros and Cons of Entryway Tension Rod Racks

Before you buy, here's a rundown of what tension rod racks are good for, and what's worth keeping in mind.

Pros

  • Installs without drilling, so you do not have to worry about restoring the wall when renting
  • If your spot's measurement falls within the supported range, no construction work is needed
  • Some products let you choose the width or height, making it easy to match your entryway
  • Adds room for shoes, coats or umbrellas with little effort

Cons

  • If the measurement of your spot (width between walls, or floor-to-ceiling height) falls outside the product's supported range, you cannot install it
  • Tension racks are not earthquake-resistant fixtures, so if you are worried about tipping or falling in an earthquake, consider other storage methods too
  • If your shoe cabinet already gives you enough storage, there is not much need to add one

First Steps After You Buy

Before installing, measure the spot where you want it (the width between walls, or the floor-to-ceiling height) and check that it falls within the product's supported range; this helps you avoid a rack that cannot be installed. Makers state weight capacity in different units (per shelf, for the whole unit), so compare the figure with the weight of what you plan to put on it. In general, avoid loading heavy items right after installation; start with lighter ones and watch how it holds so you notice any loosening early. Tension racks are not earthquake-resistant fixtures, so keep heavy items on the lower shelves.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
