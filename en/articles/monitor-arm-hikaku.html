If you're searching for a monitor arm — to raise your monitor to eye level, or to free up depth on your desk — you'll quickly run into a wall of similar-looking products that are hard to tell apart. Monitor arms broadly split into two mount types: clamp-mount (clamps onto the edge of the desk) and grommet-mount (passes through a hole drilled in the desktop) — and which one you use changes how much it affects your desk. Here we compare four products by scoring them on five criteria: weight capacity, supported screen size, warranty, mounting and extra features.

Pros and Cons of Monitor Arms

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • No monitor stand needed, freeing up desk depth
  • Fine height and angle adjustment makes it easy to match your eye level
  • Cable routing can be bundled and hidden along the arm

Cons

  • Can't be installed if your monitor's weight is close to or over the weight capacity limit
  • If your desktop is too thin or too thick, it may fall outside the clamp's supported range
  • Grommet mounting requires drilling a hole in the desktop

How to Choose a Monitor Arm: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Does Your Monitor's Weight Fit the Weight Capacity?

Check your monitor's weight first. Outside the stated range it cannot be held properly, and the arm may sag. The listed figure is a maximum, so leave some margin.

2

Check Screen Size and the VESA Standard

If the screen size or the screw-hole spacing on the back of the monitor (the VESA standard) does not match, the arm simply will not fit. Most monitors use 75×75 mm or 100×100 mm; check the manual or the label on the back.

3

Mounting: Clamp or Grommet?

A clamp grips the edge of the desk and needs no drilling. A grommet mount passes through a hole in the desktop, so it assumes drilling. If you rent, pick a model that supports clamping. All four products here are listed as supporting both clamp and grommet mounting.

4

Arm Mechanism: Gas Spring or Mechanical Spring

A spring is what lets the arm move and then hold its position. There are gas-spring types, which use gas pressure, and mechanical-spring types, which use a metal spring. In this article BONTEC is listed as a gas-spring type and COFO Pro as a double mechanical-spring type.

5

Warranty Length

An arm is something you keep for years, so look at the warranty too. The length of the maker warranty differs a lot between products.

6

Extra Features (Cables, Easy Mounting, Adjustment)

Who Monitor Arms Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, monitor arms tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to free up desk depth by skipping a monitor stand
  • Adjust your monitor's height and angle frequently
  • Want to bundle your cable routing neatly along the arm

Consider other options if you...

  • Have a monitor whose weight is at or well over the arm's weight capacity
  • Are renting and must avoid drilling holes (stick to models supporting clamp mounting)
  • Already have a monitor stand you're happy with and don't need to switch

Things to Know Before You Buy, and How to Handle Them

Small points can still lead to regret if you buy without knowing them. Here are three things worth knowing before you buy, and what to do about each.

📉

The arm may sag as you use it

An arm is held up by a spring, so if the holding force is not enough for your monitor's weight, it may sag. Among the products here, BONTEC is listed as a gas-spring type. How to adjust it differs by model, so check the manual.

🔩

Grommet mounting requires drilling a hole in the desktop

This is best avoided on a rented desk that must be restored to its original condition. If you do not want to drill, choose a clamp-only model, or one that supports both clamp and grommet mounting.

📏

If the VESA standard does not match, it cannot be mounted

If the screw-hole spacing on the back of your monitor (the VESA standard) is outside a product's supported range, the arm will not fit. Checking your monitor's VESA standard in the manual or on the back label before buying makes this easy to avoid.

Small points are easier to avoid when you know them in advance.

First Steps After You Buy

There is nothing difficult here. These three steps make post-installation mistakes easier to avoid.

1

Measure your desk thickness against the clamp's supported range

Measuring before you install helps you avoid the frustration of the arm not fitting once it arrives.

2

Check the range of motion before connecting cables

Before mounting your monitor, move the bare arm up, down, left and right to confirm the range of motion is what you expect, then connect the cables. This cuts down on redoing things later.

3

If it starts to sag, check how to adjust it

If the arm seems to be sagging slightly, check the manual for how to adjust it. The method differs by model.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
