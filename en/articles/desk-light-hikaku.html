If you're working or studying from home and your desk feels dim, searching for a desk lamp turns up all kinds of shapes and prices — clamp-mount, stand, and even monitor-mount styles — which makes it hard to tell which one actually fits your desk. Desk lamps generally fall into three types: clamp-mount (clips onto the edge of the desk), stand (a free-standing lamp you just set down), and monitor-mount (hangs on top of your monitor, leaving desk space free) — and which one you choose changes how you use your desk. Here we compare four products on five points: brightness, color temperature, energy use, setup and power, and extra features.

Pros and Cons of Desk Lamps

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Fine-grained brightness and color-temperature control lets you match the light to the task at hand
  • A monitor-mount style can be installed without taking up any desk space
  • You can choose a mounting style — clamp or stand — that suits your desk setup

Cons

  • Depending on your monitor's bezel thickness or curve, the clamp may not fit and the lamp can't be attached
  • If your room lighting is already fine, there's not much need to add one
  • High color-rendering, feature-rich models tend to cost more

How to Choose a Desk Lamp: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Decide the Mounting Style: Clamp, Stand or Monitor-Mounted

Your desk size, desktop thickness and monitor shape decide which style works. Start from whether you want to keep the desk surface clear or move the lamp around.

2

Check the Brightness (Luminous Flux)

Brightness is compared in luminous flux (lm); a bigger number means a brighter light source. The BenQ official page states brightness as a center illuminance over 1000 lux, not in lm, so it is left out of this chart (lux and lm are different units and cannot be compared directly).

3

See How Far the Color Temperature Can Change

Lower color temperatures look warmer, higher ones look whiter and bluer. If you want to change it by task or time of day, pick one with more settings.

4

How to Read Color Rendering

Color rendering shows how natural colors look under the light. Compare the same index (Ra) with Ra; when a maker uses another index (BenQ lists Rf), the numbers cannot be lined up directly.

5

Power and Power Consumption

Check whether a power adapter is included and whether the cord or cable is long enough. Lower power consumption means lower running costs (the BenQ figure is the power input maximum).

6

Extra Features

Auto-dimming, brightness memory and other handy extras, in one table.

Who Desk Lamps Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, desk lamps tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want fine control over brightness and color temperature to suit the task
  • Want to pick a mounting style — clamp, stand or monitor-mounted — to suit your desk
  • Find the room light alone leaves the desk too dark

Consider other options if you...

  • Are happy with the room lighting and do not need another light
  • Have a monitor with a thick or curved bezel that a monitor-mounted lamp cannot clamp to
  • Are put off by the price of high-CRI, feature-rich models (worth starting with a cheaper one)

Still Deciding? Check These in Order

Q1. Is your monitor bezel within the clamp's supported range?

If you are considering a monitor-mounted light, a bezel that is too thick or curved may not take the clamp — check the supported range before buying.

›
Q2. Is color accuracy the priority, or brightness and adjustment range?

For work where you need to judge document or skin tones accurately, a high-CRI model around Ra90 is the one to pick. For everyday reading and writing, prioritise the brightness and color temperature range instead.

›
Q3. Is your desk top within the clamp's supported thickness?

A clamp will not grip a desk top that is too thick or too thin, so measuring beforehand avoids a wasted purchase.

Common pitfalls to check before buying

With monitor-mount lamps like the BenQ ScreenBar Pro, if your monitor's bezel thickness or curvature falls outside the clamp's supported range, you won't be able to attach it — checking your monitor's bezel thickness before buying helps you avoid that. For products like the Lepro where the power adapter is sold separately, it's a good idea to have a compatible 5V/1A USB power source ready in advance, so you don't run into a lamp you can't power once it arrives. And for clamp-mount lamps in general, if your desktop is thicker than the clamp's supported range, it won't attach, so it's worth measuring your desk beforehand.

Best Pick by Situation

If you want to keep your desk surface clear, BenQ ScreenBar Pro is the pick. For work where color accuracy matters, go with Yamada Lighting Z-10RB. If you want to keep costs down while you try one out, Lepro is the candidate. And if you want a free-standing lamp you just set down, Iris Ohyama PDL-101-W is the candidate.

First Steps After You Buy

After setting it up, adjust the color temperature and brightness to match what you'll be doing (computer work, writing, reading, and so on), then use it for a few days before locking in your final settings — it makes the lamp easier to live with. For clamp-mount models, check right after unboxing that it's securely fastened to your desktop, and re-tighten it if you notice any wobble.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
