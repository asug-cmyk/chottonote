They are calculated mechanically by applying the criteria table at the end of the page to figures published on the manufacturers' official pages and manuals (and on retail or comparison pages where an official figure was not available). Whether it's drying laundry indoors during the rainy season, dealing with condensation in colder months, or protecting a closet or storage space from humidity, there are plenty of reasons to consider a dehumidifier. But dehumidifiers vary a lot by method, each suited to different seasons and use cases, and picking one based on the daily-capacity number alone often leads to mismatches — indoor laundry that doesn't dry as fast as expected, or a tank that fills up faster than you'd like. Here we compare four products on five criteria: dehumidifying method and capacity (the difference between compressor and desiccant types, and the approximate liters per day), laundry-drying speed, ease of maintenance (tank capacity and continuous-drain support), energy use, and extra features (clothes-drying mode, air purification, and ionizer functions).

Pros and Cons of Dehumidifiers

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Cuts down on musty odors and mold risk from indoor laundry drying
  • Helps protect window frames and wallpaper from condensation damage in the rainy season or winter
  • Models with a clothes-drying mode let you dry laundry indoors regardless of the weather

Cons

  • Compressor types can have noticeable operating noise and vibration
  • The unit auto-stops when the tank is full, so you'll need to empty it regularly or set up continuous drainage
  • Higher-capacity, more feature-rich models tend to be larger and pricier

How to Choose a Dehumidifier: 7 Points

Here are 7 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Compare Dehumidifying Power at the Same Conditions

If power is too low for the room or the amount of laundry, the humidity never comes down. But the daily capacity changes a lot with the test conditions (temperature and humidity). Here we line the products up at the same standard conditions where possible (27°C, 60% RH, 60 Hz regions). Only Iris Ohyama is listed at 20°C and 60% RH.

2

Know the Dehumidifying Methods

The method changes which season it suits, how much it warms the room, the noise and the running cost. Three of the four products here are compressor types; only Iris Ohyama is a desiccant type.

3

How Fast It Dries Laundry

For indoor drying, the time to dry 2 kg of laundry is a useful guide. These are test values the makers publish, based on the JEMA-HD090 industry standard and similar; the frequency and room size are as each maker states them.

4

Ease of Draining and Cleaning

A dehumidifier collects water, so how often you empty it and how much upkeep it needs decides whether you keep using it. We look at four things.

5

Operating Noise (Especially for a Bedroom)

Compressor types can be noisy or vibrate. If it will sit in a bedroom, check the noise of the quietest operation. Only products with a published figure are shown, and noise is not part of the Spec Score.

6

A Guide to Running Costs

Power draw alone hides differences in capacity, so we calculated the energy used per liter removed: rated power (60 Hz) × 24 h ÷ daily capacity. Real running costs depend on how you use it.

7

Extra Features

Clothes-drying mode, humidity setting, timer, child lock and other handy extras, in one table.

Who Dehumidifiers Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, dehumidifiers tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to stop the musty smell of indoor-dried laundry, and keep mould down
  • Want to prevent rainy-season or condensation damage to window frames and wallpaper
  • Want to dry laundry regardless of the weather, using a garment-drying mode

Consider other options if you...

  • Only want humidifying (these are dehumidifier-led; of the four, only the Sharp KI-TD50-W also humidifies)
  • Want to avoid the running noise and vibration a compressor type brings to a bedroom
  • Would find emptying the tank, or setting up continuous drainage, a chore

Common Annoyances and How to Handle Them (General Tips)

In general, the operating noise and vibration typical of compressor types can be noticeable at night. If it will run in a bedroom, models with an off-timer can be set to run only for a set stretch before bed, and the AEOCKY has a Sleep mode (35 dB, per the seller). Dehumidifying stops when the tank is full, so on models that support continuous drainage (Yamazen and AEOCKY), setting up a drain path to a bathroom or sink in advance saves emptying the tank. When using a clothes-drying mode, leaving enough clearance between the laundry and the unit so airflow isn't blocked is generally said to help laundry dry faster.

Best Pick by Situation

For a balance of capacity and price, Yamazen EDC-H601(B) is the pick. If raw dehumidifying power matters most, go with AEOCKY RHEA-001 (listed as 40 L/day). If you want dehumidifying, humidifying, and air purification in one unit, Sharp KI-TD50-W is the candidate. And if you also want to dry laundry in cold months, the desiccant-type IRIS OHYAMA IJD-P20-H, a type generally said to work better in the cold, is the candidate (its capacity is small).

First Steps After You Buy

The first things worth checking after setup are how the tank is installed and how the unit behaves when the tank is full (auto-stop). For models that support a continuous-drain hose, deciding on a drain path and connecting the hose right away saves a lot of ongoing maintenance effort. If you plan to use a clothes-drying mode, deciding where to hang laundry relative to the unit's air outlet ahead of time makes it easier to gauge and adjust drying efficiency. Care schedules differ by model (Yamazen advises the filter about every 2 weeks; Sharp advises the dehumidifying tray about weekly). Checking the manual up front helps prevent odors and reduced dehumidifying efficiency.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
