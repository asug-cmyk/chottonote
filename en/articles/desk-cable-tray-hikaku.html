Under-Desk Cable Tray Comparison: What Works Even in a Rental

Published: 2026-09-02 / Updated: 2026-09-02

This page participates in the Amazon Associates Program, and purchases made through product links may earn this site a referral fee.

Look under your desk and you'll probably find a tangle of power strip, charging cables, and monitor wiring — cables you have to nudge aside every time you vacuum. It looks messy, but if you're renting, drilling into a wall or desktop for a proper installation isn't really an option. A cable tray is a bar- or mesh-shaped storage piece that gathers cables together under the desk and lifts them off the floor, and whether it works in a rental depends heavily on the mounting method. Here we compare four products with an eye on whether they hold up in a rental.

Pros and Cons of Under-Desk Cable Trays

Before you buy, here's a rundown of what a cable tray gets you, and what's worth watching out for.

Pros

  • Many models use a clamp mount that needs no drilling, so you can use them in a rental without worrying about restoring the original condition
  • Weight capacity varies widely, so you can pick a model that can also hold a power strip or AC adapters
  • Lifting cables off the floor means less effort dodging them when you vacuum

Cons

  • If your desktop is less than 5cm thick, some clamp-style models physically can't attach
  • If you only have a handful of cables and zip ties or velcro straps are already doing the job, there's less need to add one
  • Screw-mount models require drilling into the underside of the desktop, making them a hard sell for renters

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Whether it can be installed without drilling (can it be used in a rental without worrying about restoring the original condition?)

2. Weight capacity (can it hold a power strip or AC adapters in addition to cables?)

3. Desktop conditions needed for installation (depth, thickness, and the material underneath the desktop can all affect whether it can be mounted)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

A complaint that shows up across clamp-mount products in general is that the clamp doesn't grip properly when the desktop's thickness falls outside the supported range. Checking the supported thickness on the product page before buying helps you avoid this. With the screw-mount FLEXISPOT, there are reports of people ordering without checking what's under the desktop and then being unable to install it. Some users have also mentioned mounting it back-to-front as a workaround to improve how it looks.

Best Pick by Situation

If you're renting and just want the simplest setup, SONATURE is the pick. If you want to stash heavier items like a power strip too, go with IMILLET. If cost comes first and your desktop has enough depth, NODOCA is the candidate. And if you own your desk, know what's underneath, and want a larger installation footprint, FLEXISPOT CMP027 fits the bill.

First Steps After You Buy

Before installing, measure your desktop's thickness, depth, and the material underneath it — doing so helps you avoid failures like not being able to mount it, or having it end up looser than expected. Before running your cables through for real, do a trial placement first, check that it doesn't get in the way of your chair or legs, and then fix it in place — that cuts down on redoing the work. If you're planning to load it with something heavy like a power strip, it's worth double-checking the weight capacity on the product page again. Our editorial team has also used a different, mesh-style cable tray, and while the mesh structure breathes well, we found that with a lot of cables it tends to poke slightly through the mesh — a point that lines up with what reviewers have said too.
