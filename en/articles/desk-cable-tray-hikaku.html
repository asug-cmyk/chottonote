Look under your desk and you'll probably find a tangle of power strip, charging cables, and monitor wiring — cables you have to nudge aside every time you vacuum. It looks messy, but if you're renting, drilling into a wall or desktop for a proper installation isn't really an option. A cable tray is a bar- or mesh-shaped storage piece that gathers cables together under the desk and lifts them off the floor, and whether it works in a rental depends heavily on the mounting method. Here we compare four products with an eye on whether they hold up in a rental.

Pros and Cons of Under-Desk Cable Trays

Before you buy, here's a rundown of what a cable tray gets you, and what's worth watching out for.

Pros

  • Many models use a clamp mount that needs no drilling, so you can use them in a rental without worrying about restoring the original condition
  • Weight capacity varies widely, so you can pick a model that can also hold a power strip or AC adapters
  • Lifting cables off the floor means less effort dodging them when you vacuum

Cons

  • If your desktop is less than 5 cm deep, some clamp-style models physically can't attach
  • If you only have a handful of cables and zip ties or velcro straps are already doing the job, there's less need to add one
  • Screw-mount models are fixed to the desktop with screws, making them a hard sell for renters

How to Choose an Under-Desk Cable Tray: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

If You Rent, Can It Go On Without Drilling?

The mounting method decides whether it works in a rental. A clamp type just grips the desktop with no drilling; a screw type is fixed to the desktop with screws, which makes it harder to choose when renting.

2

Measure Your Desktop Thickness and Depth

A clamp will not grip if the desktop thickness is outside its range. Measure your desk before you buy and compare it with the supported thickness.

3

Weight Capacity (Will a Power Strip Go On It?)

Cables alone are light, but a power strip or AC adapters need more capacity.

4

Tray Length

A longer tray holds more cables and gear, but check that it clears your legs and chair. Extendable types let you adjust the width.

5

Accessories and Features

Ties and clips in the box mean you can tidy the wiring without buying extras.

6

What to Measure Before You Buy

Most failed installs can be avoided by measuring first.

Best Pick by Situation

If you're renting and just want the simplest setup, SONATURE is the pick. If you want to stash heavier items like a power strip too, go with IMILLET. If your desktop is 18 to 32 mm thick and has enough depth, NODOCA is the candidate. And if you own your desk, know what's underneath, and want a larger installation footprint, FLEXISPOT CMP027 fits the bill.

Mounting Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

A clamp will not grip if the desktop's thickness falls outside the range on the product page, so check that range before buying. The NODOCA needs at least 5 cm of desktop depth and cannot be used on rounded or stepped edges. With the screw-mount FLEXISPOT, check the underside of your desktop before you order.

First Steps After You Buy

Before installing, measure your desktop's thickness, depth, and the material underneath it — doing so helps you avoid failures like not being able to mount it, or having it end up looser than expected. Before running your cables through for real, do a trial placement first, check that it doesn't get in the way of your chair or legs, and then fix it in place — that cuts down on redoing the work. If you're planning to load it with something heavy like a power strip, it's worth double-checking the weight capacity on the product page again.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
