Pros and Cons of Mattresses

Here's what tends to satisfy people after replacing their mattress, and what's worth checking before you buy.

Pros

  • Switching to a mattress that suits your body can ease back and shoulder pain
  • A mattress with good breathability and pressure distribution can reduce night sweats and make it easier to turn over in your sleep
  • Many ship compressed and rolled, so you can unbox and set them up right at your door

Cons

  • Since you can't lie down and test it before buying, how firm it feels varies a lot by person
  • A new-mattress smell from the compressed packaging can linger, and some products need a few hours to a day of airing out after unboxing
  • Thicker models come in larger, heavier packaging, so check your delivery access beforehand

Who Mattresses Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, these mattresses tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to switch to a firmness that suits your body and ease back or shoulder pain
  • Want better airflow and weight distribution, for night sweats and for turning over more easily
  • Want something that arrives compressed and can be set up from the front door without help

Consider other options if you...

  • Are uneasy about buying without lying on it first and would rather try one in a shop
  • Are happy with your current mattress's firmness and durability
  • Have a delivery route or a space that cannot take a thick, heavy model

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

Across mattresses in general, a common complaint is that a new-mattress smell from the compressed packaging is noticeable right after unboxing. Airing it out for a few hours to a day in a well-ventilated room usually reduces the smell significantly. Another common complaint is that the mattress feels firmer or softer than expected — an outcome that's hard to avoid given how much firmness perception varies by person. Checking the return/exchange terms and trial period in advance, or trying a similarly firm mattress in a physical store first if possible, can help reduce the chance of a mismatch. Thicker models can take longer to fully expand after being compressed, so even if it doesn't reach its full thickness right after unboxing, it's worth waiting about 24 hours before judging the feel.

First Steps After You Buy

Once you open the packaging, take the mattress out in a well-ventilated room, lay it flat, and let it sit for a few hours to a day. Compressed mattresses often haven't fully returned to their true thickness right after unboxing, and leaving them for about 24 hours brings them closer to their intended thickness and firmness. Odors can transfer from the packaging material, so airing it out with the windows open helps reduce any lingering smell. If you're using it on a bed frame, a slatted base or a breathable frame helps prevent moisture buildup and keeps the sleep feel more consistent. For products with a warranty or trial period, it's worth noting the terms (how to apply, the deadline) at the time of purchase so you're ready if you ever need them.
