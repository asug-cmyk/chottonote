If you're searching for a wireless charger to skip the hassle of plugging in a cable at your desk or bedside, you'll quickly run into everything from flat pads you just set your phone on, to stands that let you use your phone while it charges, to 3-in-1 pads that charge multiple devices at once — and the range of shapes and outputs can make it hard to choose. Wireless chargers broadly split into stand types (easy to see the screen while charging), foldable types (easy to carry), and multi-device types (charge your phone, earbuds, and smartwatch together), and which one you pick changes your day-to-day experience. Pros and Cons of Wireless Chargers

Before you buy, here's a rundown of what wireless chargers do well, and what's worth keeping in mind.

Pros

  • No cable to plug in — just set your device down to charge it
  • With a stand-type charger, you can see the screen while it charges
  • A multi-device charger lets you charge your phone, earbuds, and smartwatch all at once

Cons

  • If you want a solid magnetic hold with iPhone's MagSafe standard, a non-MagSafe model may feel like it has weaker magnetic grip
  • If you're already happy with your current charger's speed and ease of use, there's less reason to upgrade
  • Charging through a case can lose efficiency depending on how thick the case is

Who Wireless Chargers Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, wireless chargers tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to drop a phone down and charge it, without plugging a cable in
  • Want several devices charging in one spot
  • Want to see notifications and the screen while it charges

Consider other options if you...

  • Are already happy with the speed and placement of the charger you use
  • Put charging speed first, at wired fast-charge levels (wireless tends to be somewhat slower)
  • Want a MagSafe charger to hold firmly through a thick case (you would need to remove it or switch to a thinner one)

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Output (wattage) (does it directly affect charging speed, and does it match your device's maximum supported output?)

2. Compatible devices and standards (does it support your phone's standard, such as Qi2 or MagSafe?)

3. Stand stability and ease of placement (do you want to see the screen while charging, or place multiple devices at once?)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

Across stand-type chargers in general, models without magnets are often reported to lose charge depending on the angle or position of the phone. Getting into the habit of checking that the charging light is on right after you set your phone down makes it easier to notice if charging has stopped partway through. MagSafe-compatible models can feel like they have a weaker magnetic hold if you're using a thick case or one that contains incompatible magnetic material, so it's worth checking your case's compatibility before buying. For foldable, multi-device chargers, the hinge that opens and closes repeatedly tends to loosen over time, so avoiding rough opening and closing helps it last longer.

Best Pick by Situation

If you just want to keep costs down and try set-and-charge convenience, Anker PowerWave 10 Stand is the pick. For a secure magnetic hold with a MagSafe-compatible phone, go with Anker MagGo. If you want to charge your phone, earbuds, and smartwatch all together, Belkin Qi2 25W 3in1 is the candidate. And if you want the reassurance of a domestic brand balanced against price, Elecom EC-QS10BK fits the bill.

First Steps After You Buy

After setting it up, place your phone on it and check that the charging light or notification displays correctly, then watch for a few minutes to make sure charging doesn't cut out partway through. For MagSafe-compatible models, check the strength of the magnetic hold and how long it takes for charging to start with the case you normally use still attached — that will help you judge how comfortable it will be for everyday use. For multi-device chargers, try placing every device you plan to charge at once and confirm that they all start charging without interfering with each other.
