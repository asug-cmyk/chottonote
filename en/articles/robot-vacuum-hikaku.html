Pros and Cons of Robot Vacuums

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Floors get cleaned automatically while you're out or asleep
  • Schedule it to run at the same time every day and you stop having to pull out a vacuum at all
  • Higher-end models automate mopping and dust collection too, cutting down on maintenance

Cons

  • Cords and small items left on the floor need to be cleared away beforehand
  • Some models can't climb over thresholds or a raised entryway step
  • Higher-end models with an auto-empty station or mopping tend to cost more

How to Choose a Robot Vacuum: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Compare Suction by the Maximum Figure

A robot vacuum's suction (Pa) is the maximum figure the maker lists. Makers measure it differently, so treat gaps as a guide. On mostly hard floors an entry model may be enough; the more carpet or tatami you have, the more a higher figure helps.

Who Robot Vacuums Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, robot vacuums tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to remove vacuuming from your day entirely, with the floors done while you are out or asleep
  • Do not mind clearing cables and small objects off the floor before each run
  • Can put budget towards higher-end features such as automatic dust collection and mop washing

Consider other options if you...

  • Have a layout with many thresholds, a raised entryway step, or thick rug edges it may not clear
  • Have a lot on the floor and would find clearing it before every run a burden
  • Only want to try one cheaply and do not need features like automatic dust collection

Commonly Cited Complaints About Robot Vacuums, and How to Handle Them

Complaints commonly cited about robot vacuums in general include getting stuck riding up onto cords or small items such as socks left on the floor, and being unable to climb a raised entryway step or a rug's thick edge. Clearing floor obstacles before each run, and, on models that support no-go zones, setting entryways or thick rug edges in the companion app, makes these interruptions easier to avoid. On models with an auto-empty station, the noise while it pulls the dust out is generally said to be on the loud side (it varies by model), so if you don't want it running during the night, shifting the cleaning schedule to daytime hours helps. On mop-equipped models, leaving a damp mop pad attached for a long time after a run generally tends to cause odor, so it is worth removing and drying the pad promptly, or enabling the automatic mop washing and drying function on models that have one.

First Steps After You Buy

The first thing worth doing after setup is mapping your floor plan through the companion app (on models with a mapping feature). Running one full-room clean up front to build the map makes it much easier afterward to designate room-by-room cleaning or set up no-go zones. Clearing away loose cords, rug fringe, and small ornaments before that first run helps prevent tangles or collisions from stopping it early. For models with an auto-empty station or mopping support, checking the manual for how often to replace consumables such as dust bags or mop pads ahead of time makes ongoing upkeep much smoother.
