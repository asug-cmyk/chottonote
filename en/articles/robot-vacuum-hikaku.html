If you're too busy with work or living alone and can't spare the time to vacuum, or you just want to automate the floor-cleaning part of housework, you've probably searched for a robot vacuum — and run into a mess of suction-power numbers, dust-collection methods, and wide price gaps that make it hard to tell which one to pick. Pros and Cons of Robot Vacuums

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Floors get cleaned automatically while you're out or asleep
  • Schedule it to run at the same time every day and you stop having to pull out a vacuum at all
  • Higher-end models automate mopping and dust collection too, cutting down on maintenance

Cons

  • Cords and small items left on the floor need to be cleared away beforehand
  • Some models can't climb over thresholds or a raised entryway step
  • Higher-end models with an auto-empty station or mopping tend to cost more

Who Robot Vacuums Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, robot vacuums tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to remove vacuuming from your day entirely, with the floors done while you are out or asleep
  • Do not mind clearing cables and small objects off the floor before each run
  • Can put budget towards higher-end features such as automatic dust collection and mop washing

Consider other options if you...

  • Have a layout with many thresholds, a raised entryway step, or thick rug edges it may not clear
  • Have a lot on the floor and would find clearing it before every run a burden
  • Only want to try one cheaply and do not need features like automatic dust collection

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Suction power and how dust is captured (the approximate Pa rating, and whether mopping is supported)

2. Maintenance effort (whether the dust bin auto-empties, and whether the mop auto-washes and auto-dries)

3. Mapping and app features (how accurately it reads your floor plan, whether it saves multiple floor maps, and voice-control support)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

A common thread across robot vacuums in general is that they get stuck riding up onto cords or small items like socks left on the floor, or can't climb over a raised entryway step or a rug's thick edge. Clearing floor obstacles before each run, and setting entryways or thick rug edges as "no-go zones" in the companion app, cuts down on most of these interruptions considerably. On models with an auto-empty station, some users find the station's collection noise louder than expected, so if you don't want it triggering during the night, shifting the cleaning schedule to daytime hours helps. On mop-equipped models, leaving a damp mop pad attached for too long after a run tends to cause odor, so it's worth removing and drying the pad promptly when a self-washing/drying function isn't available, or simply enabling that function when it is.

First Steps After You Buy

The first thing worth doing after setup is mapping your floor plan through the companion app. Running one full-room clean up front to build the map makes it much easier afterward to designate room-by-room cleaning or set up no-go zones. Clearing away loose cords, rug fringe, and small ornaments before that first run helps prevent tangles or collisions from stopping it early. For models with an auto-empty station or mopping support, checking the manual for how often to replace the dust bags or mop pads ahead of time makes ongoing upkeep much smoother.
