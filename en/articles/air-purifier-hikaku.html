If you're searching for an air purifier because pollen or household dust bothers you, or because you've started noticing pet odor and shed hair since getting a pet, you'll quickly run into a problem: every manufacturer states room coverage and filter type differently, making it hard to tell what size you actually need for your room. Air purifiers mainly differ along three lines: room coverage (whether it has enough power for the room you'll actually use it in), filtration performance (whether it has a HEPA filter and how thorough its deodorizing function is), and ease of maintenance (how often and how much it costs to replace filters, and how easy the unit itself is to clean). Here we compare four products along these three axes.

Pros and Cons of Air Purifiers

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Reduces bothersome odors and particles like pollen, household dust, and pet smells
  • Filtration performance such as a HEPA filter makes it easier to keep your room's air clean
  • Choosing one that matches your room's coverage makes it easier to get results suited to your room's size

Cons

  • If you're already using a unit suited to your room, the benefit of upgrading is limited
  • For very small spaces like an office desk, a compact tabletop type is easier to use
  • In a living situation where you don't need an air purifier much, it becomes a lower priority

Who Air Purifiers Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, air purifiers tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Are bothered by pollen, household dust or pet odor
  • Are looking for a model whose room coverage matches the size of your room
  • Want to know the filter replacement effort and cost up front, before choosing

Consider other options if you...

  • Already have an air purifier sized to your room and are happy with it
  • Only need to cover a very small area, such as an office desk (a desktop model is easier to handle)
  • Care more about a low purchase price than about combined humidifying and deodorizing functions

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Whether room coverage matches your room's size (does it have power to spare for the room you'll actually use it in?)

2. Filtration performance and deodorizing function (whether it has a HEPA filter, and how well it handles pollen, household dust, pet odor, and the like)

3. Ease of maintenance and running cost (how often and how much it costs to replace filters, and how easy the unit and intake are to clean)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

Across air purifiers in general, common complaints include operating noise being louder than expected and filter replacement costs adding up. If the operating noise bothers you at bedtime, setting the fan to auto or quiet mode, and choosing a model with room coverage well beyond what your room actually needs, makes it easier to get enough purification even on a low setting. Filter replacement frequency and cost vary a lot by model, so checking the approximate price and replacement cycle of genuine filters before you buy helps avoid a surprise gap in running costs later. Dust buildup at the air intake reduces purification effectiveness, so it's worth vacuuming or wiping it down with a dry cloth about once a month.

Best Pick by Situation

If you want to handle humidifying and air purification in one unit, Sharp KC-J50C-H is the pick. For a large living room where odor and deodorizing matter most, go with Daikin MC556A-W. If you want a compact fit for a bedroom or single room, Levoit Core P350 is the candidate. And if value and low maintenance effort are your priority, Iris Ohyama AAP-S30C-W fits the bill.

First Steps After You Buy

Before your first run, remove the plastic bag covering the filter (purification won't work if it's left on, so this is worth double-checking). Placing the unit 10 to 20cm away from the wall, and keeping the air intake and outlet unobstructed, helps it perform at its rated level. For models with an auto mode, running it in auto for the first few days lets you get a feel for how it reacts to the air quality in your room, which makes it easier to figure out how you'll want to use it going forward.
