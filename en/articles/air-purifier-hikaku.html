Pros and Cons of Air Purifiers

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • Reduces bothersome odors and particles like pollen, household dust, and pet smells
  • Filtration performance such as a HEPA filter makes it easier to keep your room's air clean
  • Choosing one that matches your room's coverage makes it easier to get results suited to your room's size

Cons

  • If you're already using a unit suited to your room, the benefit of upgrading is limited
  • For very small spaces like an office desk, a compact tabletop type is easier to use
  • In a living situation where you don't need an air purifier much, it becomes a lower priority

How to Choose an Air Purifier: 7 Points

Here are 7 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Match the Rated Room Size to Your Room

The rated room size shows how comfortably the unit can clean a room of that size. Choose one rated above your actual room, and even a low setting can keep the air moving.

Related pickDaikin MCK706A-W（Room coverage 5/5）

2

Know the Filter and Deodorizing Methods

Pollen and dust are caught by filters; odors are absorbed by activated carbon or broken down or suppressed by discharge or ions. The products here each combine these differently.

3

Decide Whether You Need Humidifying

The Sharp and Daikin models here purify and humidify in one unit. That helps if dry air also bothers you, but it adds refilling and upkeep.

4

Operating Noise (Especially for a Bedroom)

If it will sit in a bedroom, check the noise of its quietest mode. The gap from the top setting also varies a lot by model.

Related pickDaikin MCK706A-W（Quietness 5/5）

5

Power Consumption (A Guide to Running Costs)

Power use changes a lot with the mode. If you will leave it on, look at the power drawn in the quietest mode.

Ease of Upkeep and Filter Replacement

Who Air Purifiers Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, air purifiers tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Are bothered by pollen, household dust or pet odor
  • Are looking for a model whose room coverage matches the size of your room
  • Want to know the filter replacement effort and cost up front, before choosing

Consider other options if you...

  • Already have an air purifier sized to your room and are happy with it
  • Only need to cover a very small area, such as an office desk (a desktop model is easier to handle)
  • Care more about a low purchase price than about combined humidifying and deodorizing functions

Common Concerns and How to Handle Them

With air purifiers in general, operating noise can turn out louder than expected, and filter replacement costs can add up. If the noise bothers you at bedtime, setting the fan to auto or quiet mode, and choosing a model rated well beyond your room's size, makes it easier to keep the air moving even on a low setting. Filter replacement frequency and cost vary a lot by model, so checking the approximate price and replacement cycle of genuine filters before you buy helps avoid a surprise in running costs later. Dust buildup at the air intake can reduce purification, so it's worth vacuuming or wiping it with a dry cloth about once a month.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average.
