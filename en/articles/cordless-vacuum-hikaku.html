Cordless Vacuum Comparison: Choosing by Suction Power and Runtime

Published: 2026-09-09 / Updated: 2026-09-09

This article uses affiliate programs, including the Amazon Associates Program, to introduce products.

If you're tired of unplugging and replugging as you move from room to room, or your corded vacuum has gotten too heavy and bulky to bother pulling out, you've probably searched for a cordless vacuum to replace it — and run into a mess of price ranges and suction-power specs that make it hard to tell which one to pick. Cordless vacuums mainly differ along three lines: suction power (whether it's cyclonic, and whether it has a powered brush head), runtime (how long a single charge lasts, and whether that's enough for your room size), and ease of maintenance (how easy it is to empty the dust cup and replace the filter). Here we compare four products along these three axes.

Pros and Cons of Cordless Vacuums

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • You can move between rooms and clean without unplugging and replugging
  • Lighter than a corded model, so it's easy to grab and start using right away
  • Swap in the handheld nozzle and it also works for cleaning your car interior or sofa

Cons

  • In a home with very large floor area, a corded canister vacuum can sometimes make more sense
  • A single battery charge only lasts so long, and some models aren't well suited to cleaning a large room in one pass
  • If you're already happy with the vacuum you have, there's little need to replace it

What to Look at When Choosing

We used the following three criteria for this comparison.

1. Suction power and how dust is captured (whether it's cyclonic, and whether it has a powered brush head suited to flooring and carpet)

2. Runtime per charge (whether battery life is enough for your room size and how often you clean)

3. Ease of maintenance (how easy it is to empty the dust cup, whether the filter is washable, and how easy it is to remove hair tangled in the head)

Common Complaints in Reviews, and How to Handle Them

A common thread across cordless vacuums in general is that battery life starts to feel shorter than it did when new, and hair or pet fur tangled around the head's brush causes suction to drop off. Avoiding storage in hot, humid places and not leaving the battery sitting at full charge for long stretches helps slow that degradation. Periodically cutting away tangled hair from the head's brush with scissors or similar tools helps prevent a drop in suction. For cyclonic dust cups, checking the filter each time you empty the dust and lightly tapping out any clogged dust helps maintain suction power.

First Steps After You Buy

Following the manual to fully charge the battery before your first use makes it easier to get a feel for how long the runtime actually lasts going forward. It's also worth testing the included head and nozzle attachments right after purchase — checking whether they actually reach into gaps between furniture or under the sofa at home — so you know what to expect. For models that come with a wall-mount stand, deciding on a spot near an outlet where it's easy to charge lowers the barrier to using it every day.
