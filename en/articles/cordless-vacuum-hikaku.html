Pros and Cons of Cordless Vacuums

Here's a summary of what tends to satisfy people after buying, and what's worth checking before you do.

Pros

  • You can move between rooms and clean without unplugging and replugging
  • Many models are lighter than a corded vacuum, so it is easy to grab and start using right away
  • Swap in the handheld nozzle and it also works for cleaning your car interior or sofa

Cons

  • In a home with very large floor area, a corded canister vacuum can sometimes make more sense
  • A single battery charge only lasts so long, and some models aren't well suited to cleaning a large room in one pass
  • If you're already happy with the vacuum you have, there's little need to replace it

How to Choose a Cordless Vacuum: 6 Points

Here are 6 points to check before you buy. The figures in the diagrams come from the official and retail-page specs of the products in this article.

1

Weight (Will You Lift It Often?)

If you use it on stairs or lift it often, weight decides how long you keep using it. The conditions behind each figure differ a little, so treat the numbers as a guide.

2

Run Time (How Much Will You Clean per Charge?)

With many rooms, look at how long one charge lasts. The "maximum" figure is sometimes measured without the floor head, so we compared the time with the floor head or standard nozzle.

3

Floor-Head Features

If you mostly clean flooring and carpet, the head decides how easy it is. Check for a motor-driven head, self-propelling, and tangle resistance.

4

Care (Washable Parts)

The effort at every emptying decides whether you keep using it. More washable parts make odors and clogs easier to deal with.

5

How Dust Is Collected

Cyclone, capsule (dust cup) and paper-pack types differ in emptying and upkeep. The four products here are cyclone or capsule types.

6

Extra Features and Storage

LED light, charging stand, detachable battery and other handy extras, in one table.

Who Cordless Vacuums Are a Good Fit For — and Who Should Consider Other Options

As a category, cordless vacuums tend to work well for the following people.

Good fit if you...

  • Want to move between rooms while cleaning, without replugging
  • Want a light vacuum that you can pick up and start using immediately
  • Also want to swap to a handheld nozzle for the car or the sofa

Consider other options if you...

  • Live somewhere with a very large floor area, where a corded canister vacuum makes more sense
  • Would not get through a whole large room on a single battery charge
  • Already have a corded or cordless vacuum you are happy with

Still deciding? Check these in order

Q1. What are you mainly cleaning?

If you lift it often, for example on stairs, put weight first; if you want to cover a large area in one go, put run time first. For a quick job at a desk or in a car, a light, compact model also suits.

›
Q2. How much do you want to clean on one charge?

With several rooms or a large area to cover, choose a model with a long runtime. For shorter jobs, a model with switchable power modes is enough.

›
Q3. How much maintenance effort do you want to avoid?

Whether the dust cup and filter can be rinsed, and how easily hair comes off the head, differ a lot between products — worth reading the reviews on this before buying.

Commonly Cited Complaints, and How to Handle Them

It is generally said of cordless vacuums that battery life starts to feel shorter than it did when new, and hair or pet fur tangled around the head's brush causes suction to drop off. Avoiding storage in hot, humid places and not leaving the battery sitting at full charge for long stretches helps slow that degradation. Periodically cutting away tangled hair from the head's brush with scissors or similar tools helps prevent a drop in suction. For cyclonic dust cups, checking the filter each time you empty the dust and lightly tapping out any clogged dust helps maintain suction power.

Best Pick by Situation

If you want a light secondary vacuum for quick cleanups, Makita CL108FDSHW is the pick. If strong suction and run time matter most, go with Dyson Cyclone V10 Fluffy. If light weight and easy, washable upkeep matter most, Sharp EC-FR9-B is the candidate. And if you want a stand and a well-equipped floor head, Iris Ohyama MagiCaleena AZ-SCD-123P-W fits the bill.

First Steps After You Buy

Following the manual to fully charge the battery before your first use makes it easier to get a feel for how long the runtime actually lasts going forward. It's also worth testing the included head and nozzle attachments right after purchase — checking whether they actually reach into gaps between furniture or under the sofa at home — so you know what to expect. For models that come with a charging stand, deciding on a spot near an outlet where it's easy to charge lowers the barrier to using it every day.

How the Spec Score Is Calculated

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and the overall score is the weighted average. We use the manufacturer's official figures; where the official site could not be read, we fill in from Amazon, Rakuten Ichiba, Kakaku.com and similar retail or comparison pages (only pages that match the exact model number). A criterion with no published figure is left out and the average is taken over the rest (for care and features, only items that are actually listed earn points).
